Sheet V

Sébastien Le Prestre
de Vauban, 1633–1707.

Military engineer to Louis XIV. He built or rebuilt some 300 fortifications, and wrote the method for taking them. He knew exactly how walls fail.

Born 1633
Works ~300
UNESCO 2008
A place built by Vauban is a place saved; a place besieged by Vauban is a place lost.Saying of his contemporaries

His principles, our ramparts

Three rules from the 1600s that still hold.

i.

No dead ground

Bastions were angled so that every metre of wall could be covered by fire from another. No approach went unwatched.

In Vauban

Every transaction is ruled on. There is no path to the wallet that skips the ramparts.

ii.

Defence in depth

Ditch, covered way, ravelin, curtain, then the keep. Losing one line did not lose the fort.

In Vauban

Four independent ramparts. A bad prompt that slips past the allowlist still meets the cap and the gate.

iii.

Know the siege

He wrote the definitive method of attack, and designed his forts against it.

In Vauban

Designed against how agents actually fail: loops, injected instructions, infinite approvals, leaked keys.

Chronology

A short record.

  1. 1633

    Born in Saint-Léger, Burgundy.

  2. 1655

    Made engineer to the King.

  3. 1678

    Appointed Commissary General of Fortifications.

  4. 1698

    Designs Neuf-Brisach — the octagonal fort drawn in Figure 1 of the general plan.

  5. 1703

    Made Marshal of France.

  6. 2008

    Twelve of his fortifications inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
